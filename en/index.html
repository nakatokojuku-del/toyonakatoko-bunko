The age of the gods is not a distant past. It is here, in the very midst of your day today. Nakatoko-juku is a place to touch that age once more through the study of Watarai Shinto (度會神道, the Shinto tradition of the Outer Shrine of Ise) and the practice of wagi® (和儀) — a place to rediscover the hara of the Japanese, the steady centre held deep in the belly.
Okazaki lies to the ushitora, the north-east, of the Grand Shrine of Ise. The founder was taught that the story of one family, which set out from Izumo, came to rest here at the end of a long road. As one who carries that story, he continues, in daily practice, the way of the kami that has come down from before the time of Emperor Jinmu.

Each morning we will send you a short passage that lets you touch that age of the gods. The more you receive, the more a single steady core runs through the middle of restless days.

  • Begin each morning without losing sight of your own centre
  • Find your own place within the long chain of the stories of the gods
  • Be the first to hear of offerings, festivals and classes

Four Doors

The origin of our name, the story of the founder’s family, offerings to the Grand Shrine, and learning and practice. Each is told in full in the sections of “About Nakatoko-juku”; here we simply open the doors.

  1. The name “Nakatoko”

    It comes from the words of Deguchi Nobuyoshi, a priest of the Outer Shrine of Ise, who taught “the way of nakatoko (中常) as Shinto”. This single point that is now is, just as it is, eternity — that is the name of the ground we stand on.

    Read about the name ▸Commentary: the name “Nakatoko” (Japanese) ▸

  2. The founder’s roots and Izawa-no-miya

    From the Tomi clan of the Izumo people, to the Abe clan of Ōshū, and on to Matsuura. The founder was taught the story of a family linked to Izawatomi-no-kami, who built Izawa-no-miya.

    Read the story ▸Walking the family story through the sources (Japanese) ▸Open your own map of myth (Japanese) ▸

  3. Offerings — kakechikara and sacred sake

    Bundles of rice ears, kakechikara (懸税), offered at the Kannamesai festival, and sacred sake brewed and presented. A record of offerings continued since 2014, and of service at the festivals.

    Read about the offerings ▸Okazaki and Ise — the story of kakechikara (Japanese) ▸The sacred sake trilogy (Japanese) ▸

  4. Watarai Shinto and wagi

    Lectures in which the classics are read aloud, and the practice of wagi, born of the bodily techniques of Noh and Kyōgen. A place to know with the body what the head has understood.

    Read about our learning ▸What is Watarai Shinto? ▸About the Yamatohime-no-mikoto Seiki ▸

The Toyonakatoko Bunko (豐中常文庫) is a library that Nakatoko-juku opened to copy the Watarai Shinto texts in its keeping into digital form and to hand them widely to readers today. Its first volume is the founder’s manuscript of the Yamatohime-no-mikoto Seiki (copied in Kanbun 9, 1669, and collated by Deguchi Nobuyoshi).
