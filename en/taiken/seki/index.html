柱

Three reasons to learn at this shrine

ONE
The Watarai family’s shrine —
“the bond of the place”

Seki Shrine is the shrine of the tutelary kami of the Watarai, the family of priests of the Outer Shrine. Its enshrined kami, the priest Watarai Haruhiko, served as teacher to Sugawara no Michizane, and is still remembered today as Shiratayū (白太夫). To study Watarai Shinto at the shrine where Watarai Shinto was once studied — the learning returns to the place where it was born.

TWO
Watarai Shinto —
“roots of more than 700 years”

The study of Watarai Shinto, handed down by the Watarai family of the Outer Shrine of Ise and revived by Deguchi Nobuyoshi, a priest of that family. The age of the gods is not a myth of the distant past but is present at this very moment — through the words of the classics and the founder’s story, we invite you to receive this as something about yourself, today.

THREE
Wagi —
“practice that cleanses and purifies the body (misogi, harae)”

Wagi: “practice of the hara”, born of the bodily techniques of Noh and Kyōgen. We hand on the core of an art polished over 670 years, as practice you can take up from today. To know with the body what the head has understood. With a single suriashi step, the memory of the Japanese comes back to life.

社

Seki Shrine and Nakatoko-juku

Seki Shrine stands in Yamada, at the foot of the Outer Shrine, right in front of Iseshi Station. It is the shrine of the tutelary kami of the Watarai, the family of priests of the Outer Shrine, and was also a place where Watarai Shinto was studied.

In December 2015, at this Seki Shrine, the founder held the “Yamada Hakkai-juku” (山田白魁塾), the forerunner of Nakatoko-juku. Deguchi Nobuyoshi’s “way of nakatoko”, and his feelings for the Toyomiyazaki Bunko, the place of learning Nobuyoshi founded — what now forms the core of Nakatoko-juku first began to find words at this shrine.

Nakatoko-juku, opened in Okazaki, returns after eleven years to the shrine where it all began.
From November 2026, sessions are held on the 1st of every month (in January only, on the 15th).

Both lecture and practice take place in the shrine’s hall. It is only a short walk from Iseshi Station, so you can come by train even from far away. Please make a day of it, together with a visit to the Grand Shrine.

儀

What is wagi?

Wagi (和儀) is a practice created by the kyōgen actor Sensaburō Shigeyama, drawing on the bodily techniques handed down in Noh and Kyōgen. Its purpose is to recover the hara (肚) of the Japanese in a modern body. The founder, Matsuura, learned wagi from Sensaburō-sensei in Kyoto and gained the shihan (master instructor) qualification in September 2025. Wagi is the core programme of Nakatoko-juku.

“Before learning the theory, first move” — that is what wagi practice is. When the body knows, understanding drops into the hara.

身
A beautiful body
Tanden breathing; a voice from the belly; laughter from the belly
儀
A beautiful way of living
Openly imitating an art polished over 670 years, and feeling it in the hara
礼
A happy way of living
A heart able to give thanks to the myriad kami (yaoyorozu no kami)
Breathing (tanden breathing)
Breathe out completely, then out completely, and out completely again.
At that moment, a “core” is found within you.
And with it, immunity, the autonomic nervous system and peace of mind are restored.
Stance (axis and tame)
The vertical axis linking the hyakue (the crown of the head) and the perineum. Lowering the hips a little, you understand with your body what it means to stand from the hara.
Suriashi (release, and moving the centre)
It is not the feet but the tanden that moves — by walking as if stroking the floor, the strength of the lower body awakens, and the sense of moving from the core of the body is born.
Lines and chant (utai)
Lines from kyōgen, and the chants “Denden-mushi” (the snail) and “Yukiyama” (the snow mountain). When the voice comes from the belly, there is a moment when body and words become one.
“First move, before words.” — wagi is practice for knowing with the body.
Bringing down into the body, through practice, the “way of the hara” that reached the head in the lecture — that back-and-forth is the learning of Nakatoko-juku.

道

What is the study of Watarai Shinto?

Watarai Shinto is the stream of Shinto systematised by the Watarai family of the Outer Shrine of Ise. Deguchi Nobuyoshi (1615–1690), a priest of that Watarai family, was the man who revived it, and he spoke of “the deep meaning of taking the way of nakatoko as Shinto”.

「中常ノ道ヲ神道トセルノ深意」 “The deep meaning of taking the way of nakatoko as Shinto.”
— Deguchi Nobuyoshi (Watarai Nobuyoshi), Jindai Kōjutsu-shō (神代講述鈔), as recorded by Yamamoto Hirotari

Nakatoko is Ame-no-Minakanushi-no-kami (天御中主神), one and the same as Kuni-no-Tokotachi-no-mikoto (國常立尊) under another name. Like the Pole Star at the centre of the heavens: the centre, eternal, the unmoving axis. Nobuyoshi’s words, which called that way “Shinto”, are the ground of the name Nakatoko-juku.

Lectures at Nakatoko-juku aim to receive the words of these classics as “a matter of your own hara, today”. The age of the gods is not something to look back on with nostalgia; it is present at this very moment — from that standpoint, we will go on asking these questions together, at a shrine bound up with the Watarai family.

About the founder, Shigeki Matsuura

Shigeki Matsuura (松浦茂樹)
Founder of Nakatoko-juku. He encountered Watarai Shinto in 2008 and has pursued it ever since. In Kyoto he learned wagi, the practice created by the kyōgen actor Sensaburō Shigeyama, from Shigeyama-sensei himself, and gained the shihan qualification.

In December 2015 he opened the Yamada Hakkai-juku, the forerunner of Nakatoko-juku, at Seki Shrine. On 25 May 2026 (Reiwa 8) he formally opened Nakatoko-juku in Okazaki, and holds regular lectures and practice at the Okazaki Castle Ninomaru Noh Theatre. From November 2026, sessions are also held every month at Seki Shrine in Ise.
