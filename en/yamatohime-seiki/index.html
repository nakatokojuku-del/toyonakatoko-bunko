What is the Yamatohime-no-mikoto Seiki?

The Yamatohime-no-mikoto Seiki (倭姫命世記, “The Chronicle of Yamatohime-no-mikoto”) is one of the Five Books of Shinto (Shintō Gobusho). It records, in the form of a chronicle, the wanderings of Yamatohime-no-mikoto (倭姫命), who bore the Yata no Kagami — the sacred mirror in which Amaterasu-ōmikami is enshrined — on a journey of some sixty years, until at last she fixed its place of enshrinement beside the Isuzu River in Ise.

It is thought to date from the late Kamakura period, and it was arranged and circulated as Watarai Shinto took shape among the Watarai family of the Outer Shrine. It is the founding myth of Ise Jingū, and at the same time a foundational classic of ritual, telling of the origins of the sacred meals (mike), of kakechikara and of the Kannamesai festival.

The chapter on the founding of Izawa-no-miya (leaf 25; collated with the Taisei, pp. 72–73*) records an auspicious sign: a sacred rice plant bearing a thousand ears on a single stalk, held in the beak of a white manazuru crane (白眞名鶴). Izawatomi-no-kami (伊佐波登美神), whom the founder was taught is his ancestor, built Izawa-no-miya on this spot, and the crane was enshrined as Ōtoshi-no-kami (大歳神). The same passage also records the origin of kakechikara (懸税) — hanging that rice in offering before the Great Kami — and the origin of sacred sake, brewed as clear sake by Otohime, daughter of Ōhatanushi. Here lie the founding myths of both of Nakatoko-juku’s projects: the kakechikara offerings (the Outer Shrine’s Hatsuho-biki and the Bussan Chōkenshiki at Izawa-no-miya) and the sacred sake offerings.

* The Taisei: Daijingū Sōsho: Watarai Shintō Taisei (大神宮叢書 度会神道大成), the printed edition against which our transcription was checked.

Deguchi Nobuyoshi was a priest of the Watarai family of the Outer Shrine of Ise, and a Shinto scholar of the early Edo period. He wrote foundational works of Watarai Shinto, beginning with the Nakatomi-no-harae Mizuho-shō (中臣祓瑞穂鈔). His words “the deep meaning of taking the way of nakatoko as Shinto” are also the source of the name Nakatoko-juku.

Its bond with Nakatoko-juku

Izawatomi-no-kami (伊佐波登美神), who appears in this manuscript, is the kami who built Izawa-no-miya at the command of Yamatohime-no-mikoto. The founder was taught that this “Tomi” clan belongs to the lineage of the Izumo people, and is of the same clan as the Tomi of Izumo — the source of the founder Shigeki Matsuura’s father’s line. He was taught that his father’s line runs in a single thread: the Tomi clan of the Izumo people → the Abe clan of Ōshū (descendants of the Pole Star, hokushin) → the Matsuura.

The founder, Matsuura, holds a manuscript of a classic in which the name of his ancestor is written, and for nearly ten years has continued to offer kakechikara at Izawa-no-miya, built by the kami he was taught is his ancestor. This manuscript is the one volume that gives physical proof of the heart of Nakatoko-juku’s story.
